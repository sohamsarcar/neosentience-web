Dubbing · Voice cloning · Lip sync

The language changes. So do the lips.

VAANI Studio dubs long-form video into Indian languages in the speaker's own cloned voice, then re-animates their lips to match. Every translated sentence passes a human review gate first — because for scripture, law and teaching, an approximate translation is worse than none.

The review gate

The feature nobody else has.

Every other dubbing tool voices whatever the model produced. VAANI Studio stops, and waits. An acharya, a professor, an editor reads every segment beside its original and approves it — or rewrites it. Voicing cannot begin until the last one is blessed. Try it below.

Why it exists

Built for the work everyone else skipped.

Long form

Hours, not clips

Engineered for 25-hour course libraries — resumable, per-segment, and re-voicing only what changed. The clip-oriented tools cap out at 26 minutes or drift after 90 seconds.

Their own voice

The speaker, in another language

A cloned voice carries the teacher's own timbre into the target language, so a congregation still hears the person they know — not a stock narrator.

Glossary

Sacred terms stay fixed

Deity names and doctrinal vocabulary are pinned to agreed renderings and enforced on every translation request, so terminology never drifts between episodes.

Indic-first

Tuned for how Indian speech expands

Danda-aware segmentation and silence-borrowing calibrated to Indic expansion ratios. When a line runs long, it is automatically re-tightened to fit its slot — or flagged for a human.

Ownership

It can stay in the building

Available as a self-hosted studio edition, where the voice model and the content never leave the organisation — the thing no cloud-only competitor can offer.

Honest pricing

The cost, before you run it

Per-minute cost shown on screen before a job starts, including the lip-sync estimate — chunks, speech seconds and vendor cost. No credit obfuscation.
